1. Overview

Bloom is a habit tracker. You create habits, mark them done, and watch a garden-style calendar grow. Your habits, completion history and settings stay on your device. There is no account, no custom backend and no cloud sync, and we never receive your habit data.

The app is free and shows ads through Google AdMob (Google Mobile Ads SDK): one banner at the bottom of the Today screen. In the EEA, UK and Switzerland, the app asks for consent on first launch through Google's consent form (User Messaging Platform). Ads are requested only after that consent allows ad requests. Personalized ads are used only if you consent; if you decline, ads are limited or non-personalized. Outside those regions, ads follow Google's default settings for that region, and the consent form may not be shown. If the device is offline at launch, the app uses the previously saved consent choice. Ads may use the advertising ID, device info and approximate location, depending on consent. Your habits, completions, categories, reminder times and settings are never sent to the ad network.

App identifier: Android package com.deredo.bloom.

What we collect

  • Habits you create (title, description, schedule, optional reminder time, category, color), custom categories, completion history, and appearance and reminder settings, stored only on your device
  • Small on-device flags and counters (whether you have seen the intro, and counters that decide when the app may ask for a Google Play review)
  • Google AdMob (Google Mobile Ads SDK) may use the advertising ID, device info and approximate location, depending on consent, along with app interaction and ad performance data and your consent status

What we don't do

  • No account, and no personal profile data collected by the app itself
  • No sending of your habits, history, categories, reminders or settings to us or to the ad network
  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDK of our own
  • No push notifications or push tokens (reminders are local to your device)
  • No purchases or subscriptions, and no selling of your data
  • No camera, microphone, photos, contacts or location permission from the app itself

3. Third-party services and SDKs

The app is built with open-source libraries (Expo, React Native and related packages such as expo-notifications, expo-haptics, expo-store-review, expo-router and @react-native-async-storage/async-storage). These libraries run inside the app on your device and are not configured to send your habit or settings data to us. The one third-party service that receives data is Google's advertising SDK.

Google AdMob and the User Messaging Platform (UMP): the app shows ads through Google AdMob using the Google Mobile Ads SDK (react-native-google-mobile-ads). The only ad is a banner at the bottom of the Today screen. It appears only after consent allows ad requests, and it is hidden if an ad fails to load.

The SDK's measurement start-up is delayed until the app initializes it. In the EEA, UK and Switzerland, Google's UMP SDK asks for consent on first launch through Google's consent form. Ads are requested only after that consent allows ad requests. Personalized ads are used only if you consent; if you decline, ads are limited or non-personalized. Outside those regions, ads follow Google's default settings for that region, and the consent form may not be shown. If the device is offline at launch, the app uses the previously saved consent choice. The app does not pass your habits, history, categories, reminders or settings to Google, and it does not collect an account, purchases or personal profile data of its own.

When it serves ads, and depending on consent, Google may use the advertising ID (the Android advertising ID and app set ID), device info (such as device model, operating system and SDK performance) and approximate location derived from your IP address, along with app interaction and ad performance data (such as ad impressions and taps) and your consent status. Google uses this data to deliver and measure ads, cap how often ads appear, and detect fraud. Learn more in How Google uses data in advertising, How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy.

In-app review and Rate us: after you complete habits, at a natural pause, the app may ask you to rate it using Google Play's in-app review API (Google Play services). That flow is handled by Google Play under Google's terms; the app does not send your habit data with that request. The Rate Bloom row in Settings opens Bloom's Google Play listing in the Play Store app or your browser, where the Google Privacy Policy applies.

Reminders: reminders are local notifications. The app schedules them on your device through the Expo notifications library and Android's notification system. They are not push notifications. The app does not register a push token, and no server is involved. Reminder notifications show the habit's title and description on your lock screen. Leave the description empty if you would rather not show it there.

Haptics: short vibration feedback is produced on your device by the Expo haptics library and Android. It involves no data collection.

Apart from ad, consent and store-review network use, the app is designed to work fully offline.

4. Device permissions

The app asks for a permission only when you use a feature that needs it. You can grant or revoke permissions at any time in your device settings. Denying a permission only disables the related feature. Bloom does not ask for camera, microphone, photos, contacts or location permissions, and its configuration blocks the overlay and external storage permissions.

5. Data retention

Your habits, history, categories and settings stay on your device until you delete them in the app, clear the app's data or uninstall the app. We keep no copies.

Data collected by Google through the ads SDK is held by Google and retained under Google's own terms and policies (see the Google Privacy Policy). We do not receive it in a form that identifies you and cannot delete it for you.

Your device's operating system may include Bloom's data in device backups that you control (for example, Google device backup on Android). Those backups are handled by Google under your account and its privacy policy. We have no access to them.

6. Data security

Your data stays in the app's private storage area on your device, which the operating system protects from other apps. Its protection also depends on your device's security, such as your screen lock and device encryption. We recommend you keep your device locked and up to date. Your habits and settings are never sent over the network by the app. Google states that data collected by its ads SDK is encrypted in transit.

7. Your rights and choices

Because Bloom keeps your habit data only on your device, you control it directly and we hold no copy of it. Depending on where you live, laws such as the EU/UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) or the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA/CPRA) give you rights to access, correct, delete or port your personal data, to object to or restrict processing, and to withdraw consent. In this app:

  • Access and correction: the data the app stores is shown in the app, and you can edit it wherever the app allows editing.
  • Deletion: delete single habits, use Settings → Reset data, clear the app's data or uninstall the app (see section 8).
  • Ad consent: in the EEA, UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form is shown on first launch. Personalized ads are used only if you consent; if you decline, ads are limited or non-personalized. You can change or withdraw your consent at any time in Settings → Privacy options (where that entry is shown). You can also uninstall the app, or reset the advertising ID in Android Settings → Google → Ads.
  • Advertising ID: on Android, go to Settings → Privacy → Ads (or Settings → Google → Ads) to reset or delete your advertising ID.
  • Reminders and permissions: turn reminders off in Settings, or revoke the notification permission in your device settings, at any time.
  • No sale: we do not sell your personal information.

If you have a question about your rights, contact us at the address below. Because we receive no habit data from the app, we cannot look up or recover data stored on your device. For data held by Google, see Google's privacy controls.

8. How to delete your data

Bloom has no account, so there is no account to delete. To delete your data:

  1. Delete one habit: open the habit and delete it. Its history is also removed.
  2. Delete all habits and history: open the Settings tab → Reset data → confirm Reset. This removes every habit and its history. Your appearance and reminder settings are kept.
  3. Clear everything: on Android, open Settings → Apps → Bloom → Storage → Clear storage. This deletes all of Bloom's data, including settings, while keeping the app installed.
  4. Uninstall: uninstall Bloom to remove the app and its data from the device.
  5. Ad data: reset or delete your advertising ID as described in section 7. Data already collected by Google is handled under Google's terms.

9. Children's privacy

Bloom is not directed at children under 13 (or under the age of digital consent where you live), and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. The app shows ads and is not part of any family or children's program. If you believe a child has provided personal information through the app, contact us and we will help remove it.

Tracking here means linking data collected in this app with data from other companies' apps, websites or offline properties for targeted advertising or advertising measurement, or sharing it with data brokers. Your habits, history, categories, reminders and settings are never shared with Google or any ad network. See how Google uses data in advertising.
